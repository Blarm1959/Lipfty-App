How to play

Lipfty

A two-player strategy game with shared pieces: neither player owns a colour. In the app the board is set up for you; the setup notes below are for playing with a real board.

What you need

An 8×8 board and 24 pieces, 12 of each colour. An official Lipfty set is red and blue, just as a chess set is black and white: a red-and-blue board with 12 red and 12 blue pieces.

No Lipfty set? Use an ordinary chess or draughts board and the 24 draughts pieces (most chess sets come with them). Any two colours work if you can tell them apart.

Set up

Play on the inner 6×6, which starts empty. The outside ring holds the reserve: put the 24 pieces round it in any order, leaving the four corners empty.

The big idea

Your opponent picks your colour. Each turn you are handed one reserve piece, and you must play that colour. When your turn ends, you pick a reserve piece and hand it to your opponent.

Starting: the swap rule

The other player hands the starter the first piece and the starter places it. The other player then chooses: carry on (they are handed a piece and play next) or swap sides (they take over the starter's position, hand the starter a piece, and the starter plays next). The first piece stays where it is. So place your first piece somewhere fair: if it helps the player who placed it, your opponent will simply swap.

On your turn

Use the colour you were handed and do one of these:

Place: put the handed piece on any empty square in the 6×6. This is always allowed.

Move (Standard/Extreme): move a board piece of the handed colour one square in any direction (including diagonally) into an empty square.

Jump (Standard/Extreme): leap a board piece of the handed colour over one adjacent piece of the other colour into the empty square directly beyond, in any direction. Only one jump at a time.

You may not Move or Jump the exact piece your opponent just moved on their last turn.

After a Move

Keep the piece you were handed. Your opponent chooses a colour, the same as your piece or the other one, takes a piece of that colour from the reserve and places it. If that doesn't win, you place the piece you were holding. Then, as normal, you choose a piece for your opponent, and they take a normal turn.

After a Jump

Lift off the piece you jumped over. You now hold two pieces, one of each colour: the one you were handed and the jumped one. Your opponent chooses one of them and places it on any empty square. If that doesn't win, you place the other one. Then, as normal, you choose a piece for your opponent, and they take a normal turn.

Careful: never leave three of a colour in a row with a gap after a Move or Jump. Your opponent can pick that colour and finish the line themselves.

Winning

Make a winning pattern of one colour and you win at once, whoever the pieces “belong” to. Check after every placement, Move, Jump and redeploy. If a Move or Jump wins, skip the steps that normally follow it.

Winning patterns (four pieces of the same colour): four in a row horizontally, vertically or diagonally; in Extreme also a 2×2 Square or a spaced Square (four corners of a larger square lined up with the board).

Game versions

Learning: Place only. Four in a row: horizontal, vertical or diagonal.
Standard: Place, Move and Jump. Four in a row. The usual game.
Extreme: As Standard, plus 2×2 Squares and spaced Squares also win.

Ending the game

One colour runs out: as soon as the reserve has no pieces of one colour left, Move and Jump stop. Place the remaining reserve pieces one per turn. If a Move is already being settled, finish it first.

Draw: if all 24 pieces have been placed and nobody has made a winning pattern, the game is a draw.

First game?

Start with Learning to get used to being handed your colour, then try Standard. You can play against the computer or pass-and-play with a friend.

In the app: Undo and chess clock

The optional chess-style clock can give each player 5, 10, 15, 30 or 60 minutes, with an optional 5- or 10-second increment. Only the player currently making the decision has their clock running. Running out of time loses the game. Undo restores the clocks with the restored decision.

Swap rule

Carry on, or swap sides?

The first piece has been placed.

Carry on: you are handed a piece and take the next turn.

Swap sides: you take over the other player's position. You hand them a piece and they take the next turn. The first piece stays where it is.
