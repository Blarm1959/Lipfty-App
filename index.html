Start: the inner 6×6 is empty. Each player holds one Red Jump and one Blue Move special. Choose the first ordinary reserve colour for the opponent; the special pieces do not depend on that choice.

Your turn: use the handed colour to Place, Move or Jump. Completing four is illegal until both your specials have returned to the outside corners.

Every Jump: opponent places jumped piece → jumper places handed piece → opponent's turn.

Win: once qualified, complete four in a horizontal, vertical or diagonal line.

How to play

Lipfty

Current v20 rules (Markdown) · Older v19 PDF (previous Jump order)

Objective. First complete a Move and a Jump and return BOTH specials to the outer corners. Until then it is illegal to create four of either shared colour in a line (Easy) or a tight 2×2 square (Standard). Once both specials have returned, such a pattern wins. Larger spaced squares never count.

Setup. The inner 6×6 starts empty. Place 24 ordinary reserves around the outside edges. Each player starts holding one Red special (Jump) and one Blue special (Move), leaving all four outside corners empty. The opening colour chooser chooses an ordinary colour for the first placer; that colour does not assign special pieces.

First placement. The opponent places the first ordinary piece of the chosen colour. The chooser then selects Accept or Take position. The first piece stays where placed; both players retain their Red Jump and Blue Move specials. When the computer makes this decision, a popup explains the result; press OK to continue. Subsequent ordinary colours remain opponent-selected.

Normal play. The opponent selects an ordinary reserve colour. You may Place, Move an unpinned board piece of that colour by one adjacent cell, or Jump over an adjacent unpinned piece of the opposite colour into an empty cell. Your first Move earns Blue and your first Jump earns Red, in either order. The earned special returns automatically after all compulsory placements. An action creating four is illegal until both specials are already back; the qualifying action itself cannot create four.

Move consequence. The opponent chooses and places another ordinary reserve piece; the mover places the exact held piece. A Move requires another ordinary reserve besides the held piece. Move and Jump remain legal when one ordinary reserve colour runs out, as long as the selected action is otherwise legal.

Jump consequence (v20, EVERY Jump). The jumped piece is removed temporarily. The opponent must place that exact jumped piece first. The jumper then places the exact originally handed reserve piece. The opponent takes the next normal turn. There is no piece-order choice. Final Four pieces are not used during compulsory placements. The first successful Jump still earns the Red special, returned only after the compulsory placements; later Jumps do not earn another.

Deadline and Final Four. Keep Move and Jump available while ordinary reserves remain, subject to the usual legal restrictions. After the final ordinary reserve is placed, only a player who has completed both actions can win by opponent qualification failure. If neither player qualified, there is no winner. If both qualified, play the four returned outside corner specials as pinned Final Four pieces.

Illegal patterns and compulsory placements. Before both your specials have physically returned, you cannot create four of either colour in a line (or a tight square in Standard), whether by Place, Move, Jump or a compulsory placement. A Jump is judged after removing the jumped piece. Three in a row is legal. If every remaining placement square is forbidden during a compulsory placement, you lose immediately, even if the opponent has not qualified. On an ordinary turn a legal Move or Jump is an alternative; you only lose if no legal action remains. A qualified player may complete a winning pattern; a win ends compulsory placements. The moved/jumped piece retains its one-turn protection.

Continuing. New game starts a fresh game with the reserves and corners reset. Applying Settings also starts a new game. Clocks reset; individual-game statistics are retained.

Computer play speed. In Settings choose Immediate, 1, 2 or 3 seconds (2 seconds by default). Each computer board action, including each compulsory placement, is delayed separately. Human actions are not delayed. The clock and Undo otherwise work as before.

Clock and Undo. Time expiry ends the game; under the v19 qualification rule an opponent who has not completed both actions does not receive a victory. Undo restores position, clock and statistics and returns to a human decision against the computer.
